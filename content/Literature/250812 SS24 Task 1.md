### Solution
#### a) Probability of Viewing the "Checkout" Page

To calculate the probability that a customer viewed the "checkout" page, we use the **Law of Total Probability**. The resulting probability is known as the **Evidence**.

The formula is:

$$
P(\text{Checkout}) = P(\text{Checkout} | \text{Purchase}) \cdot P(\text{Purchase}) + P(\text{Checkout} | \text{no Purchase}) \cdot P(\text{no Purchase})
$$


Given:

* **Prior Probability** of a purchase, $P(\text{Purchase})$:


	$$
	P(\text{Purchase}) = \frac{12}{60} = 0.20
	$$


* **Prior Probability** of no purchase, $P(\text{no Purchase})$:


	$$
	P(\text{no Purchase}) = \frac{60 - 12}{60} = \frac{48}{60} = 0.80
	$$


* **Likelihood** of viewing the checkout page given a purchase, $P(\text{Checkout} | \text{Purchase})$:


	$$
	P(\text{Checkout} | \text{Purchase}) = 0.85
	$$


* **Likelihood** of viewing the checkout page given no purchase, $P(\text{Checkout} | \text{no Purchase})$:


	$$
	P(\text{Checkout} | \text{no Purchase}) = 0.15
	$$



Now, we can calculate the **Evidence**:

$$
P(\text{Checkout}) = (0.85 \cdot 0.20) + (0.15 \cdot 0.80) = 0.29
$$


The probability that a customer viewed the "checkout" page is **0.29**.

#### b) Probability of Purchase Given Checkout Page View

To calculate the probability that a customer buys something if they viewed the checkout page, we use **Bayes' Theorem**. This term is called the **Posterior Probability**.

The formula is:

$$
P(\text{Purchase} | \text{Checkout}) = \frac{P(\text{Checkout} | \text{Purchase}) \cdot P(\text{Purchase})}{P(\text{Checkout})}
$$


Using the values from part (a):

$$
P(\text{Purchase} | \text{Checkout}) = \frac{0.85 \cdot 0.20}{0.29} = \frac{0.17}{0.29} \approx 0.5862
$$


Rounding to the second decimal, the posterior probability is **0.59**.

#### c) Bayesian Risk Decision Threshold

To incorporate the specified costs into the Bayesian risk decision, we need to calculate the threshold $\theta$ for the given likelihood ratio test.

The decision rule is:

$$
\text{LR}(\text{Checkout}) = \frac{P(\text{Checkout} | s=\text{Purchase})}{P(\text{Checkout} | s=\text{no Purchase})} > \theta
$$

Decide for $s=\text{Purchase}$, otherwise for $s=\text{no Purchase}$.

The threshold $\theta$ is calculated using the formula for a two-class Bayes risk decision:

$$
\theta = \frac{\lambda_{12} - \lambda_{22}}{\lambda_{21} - \lambda_{11}} \cdot \frac{P(s=\text{no Purchase})}{P(s=\text{Purchase})}
$$


Let's define the states and the loss function $\lambda(a|s)$, where `a` is the decided class and `s` is the true class:
* $s=1$: Purchase
* $s=2$: no Purchase
* $\lambda_{11}$ and $\lambda_{22}$ are costs for correct decisions, which we assume to be **0**.
* $\lambda_{21} = \lambda(\text{decide no Purchase} | s=\text{Purchase})$ is the cost of misclassifying a buyer. Let's set this to a base cost `k`.
* $\lambda_{12} = \lambda(\text{decide Purchase} | s=\text{no Purchase})$ is the cost of misclassifying a non-buyer. The problem states this is **200 times worse** than the other error, so $\lambda_{12} = 200 \cdot k$.

Substituting these values into the threshold formula:

$$
\theta = \frac{200k - 0}{k - 0} \cdot \frac{P(\text{no Purchase})}{P(\text{Purchase})} = 200 \cdot \frac{0.80}{0.20}
$$


$$
\theta = 200 \cdot 4 = 800
$$


The numerical value of the threshold $\theta$ is **800.00**.
