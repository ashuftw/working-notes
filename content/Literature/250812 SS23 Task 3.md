#### a) Confusion Matrix

To create the confusion matrix, we count the pixels in the 12x12 grid based on the ground truth (blue stars) and the prediction (green circles). The positive class is the digit "7", and the negative class is the background.
   - Total Pixels = 144
   - TN = 144 - TP - FP - FN = 144 - 21 - 2 - 11 = **110**

The confusion matrix in the format $(\begin{smallmatrix} TP & FP \\ FN & TN \end{smallmatrix})$ is:
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 21 & 2 \\ 11 & 110 \end{pmatrix} $$

---

#### b) Intersection over Union (IoU) and Accuracy (ACC)

Based on the values from the confusion matrix, we can calculate the IoU and Accuracy.

* **Intersection over Union (IoU):** This measures the overlap between the predicted and actual positive regions.
    * **Formula:**
        $$ \text{IoU} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN} $$
    * **Calculation:**
        $$ \text{IoU} = \frac{21}{21 + 2 + 11} = \frac{21}{34} \approx 0.6176 $$
        Rounded to the second decimal, **IoU = 0.62**.

* **Accuracy (ACC):** This measures the proportion of all correctly classified pixels.
    * **Formula:**
        $$ \text{ACC} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + FP + FN + TN} $$
    * **Calculation:**
        $$ \text{ACC} = \frac{21 + 110}{21 + 2 + 11 + 110} = \frac{131}{144} \approx 0.9097 $$
        Rounded to the second decimal, **ACC = 0.91**.

---

#### c) Suitable Evaluation Metric

For semantic segmentation tasks, **Intersection over Union (IoU) is the more suitable measure**.

**Reasoning:**
The task of semantic segmentation often involves highly **imbalanced classes**, where the background (negative class) dominates the image. The hint about segmenting a small person in a large street scene illustrates this perfectly.

* **Accuracy (ACC)** is misleading in such cases. A model that predicts every single pixel as "background" would achieve a very high accuracy because the number of True Negatives (TN) would be enormous, overwhelming the other terms (TP, FP, FN) in the calculation. This high score would fail to reflect that the model completely missed the object of interest.

* **Intersection over Union (IoU)**, on the other hand, is specifically designed to handle this imbalance. Its formula, $\frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}$, **completely ignores the True Negatives (TN)**. It focuses solely on the performance related to the positive class (the object being segmented), providing a much more meaningful score of how well the prediction overlaps with the ground truth. Therefore, it is the standard and more robust metric for evaluating segmentation quality.
