## Some Background

Between 2015 and 2017, I attended a prep school that felt like a gulag designed to churn out candidates for India’s top engineering schools. It didn’t take me long to realize I wasn’t cut out for that kind of rigor, but I pressed on, determined to make it. Stories floated around about a friend’s friend—someone who’d been ridiculed as an idiot back home—who had worked relentlessly and finally succeeded. Those tales were equal parts intimidating and inspiring.

I still remember getting my first phone. I was 12, and it was just a basic, dumb phone. But the excitement of talking to friends felt almost magical. It was my first real foray into texting and non-face-to-face communication. Until then, I hadn’t really thought about it—at least not on a deeper, meta level. For a brief moment, it felt like a whole new world existed beyond the physical space I inhabited.

That sense of detachment from the physical world became even more pronounced during my two years at prep school, where I had to move away from home. Our access to the outside world was tightly controlled—we were allowed to make phone calls only three times a day: before school, during lunch, and at dinner. Those brief calls became lifelines in an otherwise isolating environment. Other than my calculator, I didn’t have access to any electronic devices.
